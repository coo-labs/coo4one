1. Dream-system thinking & modular software (the dominant theme)
[H] “The Case for Modularity: Why Modern Software Feels Broken” — dream-system
spec / frustration with current tools > “My task manager doesn’t talk to my notes. My files,
locked in an impenetrable cloud ecosystem, refuse to cooperate with the apps I need. It’s not that
these tools are bad; it’s that they feel indifferent to me, their user… the tools we use to think,
create, and organize our lives have become a barrier instead of an enabler.”
[H] “We need more modular software” — dream-system spec (cross-tags with PKM frustra-
tion) > “Most software contains three elements - data (content), data control (query, modification,
organization), and display. In an ideal modularized software world, each of these functions would
be performed by separate services… I jump from one PKM app to another, because each shines in
some aspects and falls flat in others… You want a tagging system? You can use some global tagging
system that applies to modules and content of every kind - emails, tasks, documents, etc. No more
having to manually construct ‘Project Silly Potato’ as an explorer folder, an email collection, a
Heptabase board/tag, a slack channel, etc…” — References Ink & Switch Embark essay; quotes a
Tana user expressing the same frustration.
[AN: Notes] “The Unfulfilled Vision of Malleable Software” — dream-system spec / anti-
feature > “Kay and his peers aimed to give that power to end users, enabling them to compose
their own applications… Yet, this vision remains largely unrealized… It feels like the commercial
minds and the idealistic minds at Apple are in constant conflict… Shortcuts is Turing complete!
Imagine if I could assign properties to shapes in Freeform and use Shortcuts actions to manipulate
them… most iPad apps being smaller and more focused, providing structured data storage and
common actions—each building on others, just like we do with programming libraries. Why are
all apps silos?”
[AN: Notes] “I’ve been thinking about this issue of app functionality, modularity…” —
duplicate of the Heptabase essay, same content.
[AN: Notes] “The app-based model has made project-based work difficult” — dream-
system spec / anti-feature > “Each app has its own isolated organizational system… there is no
underlying connective tissue - the system doesn’t give you any way to quickly retrieve and bring
together browser bookmarks or recently annotated pdfs about Project X… We lost something
important when the industry moved away from filesystem-based organization… We need better
connective tissue and meta-organizational layers instead of just going back.”
[AN: Notes] “Potential Solutions for Software Modularity” — dream-system spec > Lists:
protocol-based computing, federated/decentralized models, content-addressable data, capability-
based security, local-first software, mesh computing.

4. Workflow — how Ven actually works now
[AN: Archive / Notes] “Daily planning” — workflow description (high-fidelity) > Routine:
Check Calendar → Clear Email Inbox (Mail-to-Things forwarding) → Clear Things3 Inbox (clar-
ify each item) → Review Today List → Review Anytime List → Review Reader (saved readings)
→ Review Raindrop bookmarks. Tips include: “Today is for commitments only”, “Use Hookmark
to hook projects to their files”, “Anytime is for actions I need/want to do soon. Everything else
is in Someday.” This is the concrete current toolset: Things3, Apple Mail, Mail-to-Things,
Hookmark, Raindrop, Reader.
[AN: Notes] “On time management | What’s new” — workflow philosophy (saved from Terry
Tao’s blog with personal commentary) > “Make an effort to stick to an organizational system that
is effective, evolves naturally, and not too ambitious. Sticking fully to a simpler system is better
than a half-hearted compliance with a complex system. Do not obsess over such systems and
devote no more than 5% of your time to managing them. They should free memory and worry and
get out of the way. Make space for serendipity by sometimes abandoning such personal rules.”
[AN: Notes] “I am a creature of momentum, not habit” — self-knowledge — title alone is a
strong design constraint: routines must support momentum, not rigid habit chains.

Cross-cutting observations
1. The dream-system has been articulated repeatedly over years, and is consistent. Mod-
   ular, protocol-based, canvas-spatial, reactive, with cross-app tagging/linking. Inspirations are
   stable: Bret Victor, Dynamicland, Ink & Switch, Ted Nelson, Smalltalk/HyperCard, Heptabase,
   Excalidraw, Figma.
2. iPad is the contested device. Mac works; iPad almost-works-but-doesn’t. coo4one’s iOS
   surface is the hardest design problem.
3. Current concrete toolset = Things3 + Apple Mail + Hookmark + Raindrop + Reader +
   Heptabase + Zotero + Apple Notes + Apple Freeform + Shortcuts. Ven is not in Notion/
   Obsidian/Roam/Tana (no notes about them; the “Notion” search hits are about notion of in
   physics writing) — the search returned zero matches for Obsidian. Apple Notes itself remains
   his fast-capture layer.
4. AI is treated both as productivity-tool and as relational presence. The MyBoyfriendIsAI /
   Anthropic-funeral / RPG-with-LM-PCs cluster matters: coo4one should not adopt a coldly
   utilitarian persona by default.
5. Continuity / session-handoff is a named pain point. The “Continuation Summary AI
   prompt” + April 20 manifesto + nightly-COO design pattern converge on this. coo4one’s
   memory architecture is the load-bearing design decision.
6. Anti-features Ven would notice immediately: dependency hell, config sprawl, “yet another
   silo”, tools that demand >5% maintenance time, distracting IDE chrome, broken iPad parity.
