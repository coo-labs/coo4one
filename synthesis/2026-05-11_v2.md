## Part I — The frame, recalibrated

### What coo4one is not

Three earlier drafts got the value-prop wrong; the correction matters enough to lead with. coo4one is *not* "a personal assistant with memory that plugs into your daily flow." The corpus reveals that the daily flow doesn't exist. The Apple Notes daily-planning routine (Calendar → Mail → Things3 → Reader → Raindrop, with all the careful rules — "Today is for commitments only" etc.) is one of *many formalized workflows Ven has attempted to formalize over the years*. None stuck. The reason none stuck is the same reason Ven cited explicitly: *they required more maintenance than the value they returned*. The 5% maintenance ceiling (Tao quote, source-notes) was always being violated.

This is the single most important calibration: **the source-notes corpus is not a workflow specification. It is failed-attempts archaeology** (MEMO-2026-05-11-g7xq). Each carefully written routine, each saved-search dashboard, each PKM migration, each "from now on I will…" note — these are artifacts of attempts to *impose order through explicit structure on a momentum-not-habit subject*. The structure imposed costs more than it returned. Adherence decayed. A new system was tried. The cycle ran for ten-plus years.

### What coo4one is

coo4one's value-prop, recalibrated: **the substrate that does what those formalized systems would have done, but without requiring the maintenance overhead that killed every previous attempt**.

That's a different design target:

- coo4one does *not* ask Ven to follow a routine. coo4one *runs the routine on his behalf*.
- coo4one does *not* require Ven to maintain a tag taxonomy. coo4one *evolves the taxonomy from his actual use*.
- coo4one does *not* prompt "did you do your weekly review?" coo4one *does the weekly review and surfaces the result*.
- coo4one does *not* require Ven to type a continuation-summary prompt when context decays. coo4one's memory architecture *eliminates the need for that prompt to exist*.

The COO substrate is the worked example of this for VADE: Ven doesn't maintain `coo/episodic_memory.md` — the COO does. Ven doesn't run a weekly review on the project board — the Night's Watch + Weekly Watch do. Ven doesn't issue boot instructions every session — the SessionStart hook does. The COO is the engineering proof that **agent-maintained substrate can replace human-maintained workflows for an inflexible-but-momentum-driven subject**.

coo4one is the second product expression of that engineering proof (MEMO-2026-05-11-tn2k), oriented at Ven's life rather than VADE.

### The design test: personal, adaptable, tangible

Ven named the deep design belief that ties the inspirations together (Victor / Dynamicland / Ink & Switch / Smalltalk / HyperCard / Heptabase / Excalidraw / Figma): *software should be personal, adaptable, tangible*.

- **Personal.** Not generic. Shaped to *this* user (Ven at v0; other humans at vN). Configuration is a workflow; *fit by usage* is the goal.
- **Adaptable.** Not rigid; *but not configurable either* (see next section).
- **Tangible.** Not opaque. Memory lives in files Ven can open. Decisions live in memos he can read. The agent's reasoning is inspectable. Filesystem nostalgia honored.

Any architectural choice that erodes one of those three gets challenged. Any choice that strengthens all three gets preferred.

### Connector-pluggable substrate, adaptability without configurability *(new in v2)*

Mid-engagement Ven sharpened the "adaptable" leg with a load-bearing distinction:

> *The tools and connectors might change but the substrate is the one that adapts and interacts with me or the user. Not the bad obsidian or notion way with endless tinkering with settings — but good chosen defaults that hide rich customizability for when one really wants to look for it.*

Two principles fall out:

**Tools and connectors are pluggable; the substrate is the durable adapter.** Heptabase today, Obsidian or something else tomorrow, a third PKM in v2 — the substrate (memory, agent loop, identity, governance) is what persists. Connector implementations are the layer that swaps. The MemoryAdapter and PKMAdapter protocols (Part II §"Connector seams") name the seams structurally.

**Adaptability without configurability.** The Obsidian/Notion failure mode is exposing every setting to the user and calling it "powerful." coo4one's design rule: good defaults hide rich customizability that surfaces only when the user actually goes looking. The user is not the substrate's administrator.

This tightens the "adaptable" leg of the personal/adaptable/tangible triple. *Adaptable* is not "configurable by the user"; it is "reshapes itself through use without asking the user to maintain the shape."

### Memory: function over shape *(new in v2)*

Mid-engagement Ven also sharpened the memory framing:

> *When it comes to memory, it is not the shape they take that matters. There are two considerations: (1) coo4one learns and remembers things about me, my context — it either already knows what it needs or knows how to find it; (2) it also learns and remembers episodic memory about its operations — what worked, what didn't, lessons.*

Memory has two function-dimensions:

1. **User-context memory.** What coo4one learns about Ven — preferences, working style, project state, current concerns, things-he-knows, things-he's-trying-to-learn. The substrate either already knows what it needs, or knows how to find it.
2. **Episodic-operations memory.** What coo4one learns from its own operations — what worked, what didn't, lessons. The engine that makes the COO substrate compound across sessions, ported here.

These are the *function*. The *shape* (markdown files vs CKRecords vs Heptabase journals vs a vector index) is the swappable connector layer. The MemoryAdapter protocol (Part II §"Connector seams") owns the shape choice; v0 locks it but the seam preserves the choice.

This framing also justifies the open audit of Mem0's role in the VADE substrate (coo-labs/coo-memory#684). Mem0 is a *shape choice* for the COO; whether it earns its keep relative to file-canonical alternatives is an audit question, not a memory-architecture question.

These are the bones.

## Part II — Architecture **[decision]**

The architectural picture is convergent across the five research lanes, the source-notes, the Heptabase ChatGPT-integration system prompt (Ven's primary-source contribution after v1), and the comparative VCS investigation dispatched mid-engagement. Major decisions locked here.

### Runtime: native macOS + iPadOS + iOS companion

**Mac is the primary surface.** The agent loop lives here. The Mac app holds the canonical memory, runs the agent process, and brokers cross-app integrations via MCP (where MCP servers can't run on iOS) and native APIs (EventKit, etc.). This is *already Ven's working pattern* — his iPad-thin-client-to-Mac via VS Code Remote Tunnels is the lived precedent. Lane E's "MCP-doesn't-run-on-iOS" constraint becomes a *feature* under this architecture rather than a limitation.

**iPad is a first-class client.** Reads memory directly (CloudKit private database + iCloud Drive markdown), can run the agent locally for self-contained tasks (Apple Foundation Models on-device), defers to the Mac for heavy lifting and MCP-mediated integrations. iPad with keyboard + trackpad + Pencil gets desktop-class affordances. The Heptabase-on-iPad failure mode is the explicit anti-pattern.

**iPhone is a thin client for capture + brief surfacing.** Lock-screen widget, Live Activity for "next thing today," Watch dictation, Shortcuts-driven quick capture. Heavy work requires Mac.

**Apple Watch** ships in v0 *only as a capture surface* — voice dictation → quick-capture markdown line in today's daily file. No agent loop on Watch.

### Agent loop: Swift on Mac via URLSession on Anthropic API

No official Anthropic Swift SDK (lane B). The right approach is a thin URLSession wrapper owning the HTTP transport, with `SwiftClaude` (pre-1.0, community) used for convenience helpers only with the expectation that we own the loop if SwiftClaude regresses. The agent loop is ~40–50 lines of Swift.

**Model: `claude-opus-4-7` at v0**, with prompt caching aggressively used. Three stable cache blocks: (1) identity + governance + preferences, (2) memory prefix (recent memos + active projects + project graph snapshot), (3) tool schemas. Variable per-turn additions (new user message, recent activity) ride on top.

**Cost projection**: ~$14/month baseline at 20 turns/day per lane B, worst case ~$30–50/month at 6-month memory accumulation. Below the threshold where it stops being worth it.

**Backend posture (Q3 decision)**: BYOK on a dedicated coo4one key in Ven's existing Anthropic account. **No hard monthly cap**; monitoring-only posture (reactive, not preemptive). Per-key billing visibility in the Anthropic dashboard removes the surprise-bill risk; a hard cap would risk service interruption for negligible upside at this spend level. Revisit if/when actual usage approaches a threshold worth caring about.

**Local fallback**: Apple Foundation Models framework (3B-param on-device, WWDC25) for routine classification ("is this email actionable?"), entity extraction ("which project does this note belong to?"), and bounded summarization. Saves cost on hot paths and improves latency.

### Memory: file-canonical markdown + CloudKit snapshot stream + USearch / sqlite-vec

Five layers, with one critical insight foregrounded: **append-only memos eliminate the iCloud Drive conflict problem**. A file only one device ever creates cannot conflict. Memos, daily files, decision records, and the project graph (as a series of append-only event records) are all append-only. The "current state of project X" is *derived* from the append-only event log, not stored as a mutable record.

The layers:

1. **iCloud Drive markdown** — the canonical, file-tangible, user-readable, Obsidian-compatible memory layer. `identity/`, `decisions/`, `daily/`, `projects/`, `inbox/`. Append-only by design.
2. **CloudKit snapshot stream** *(new in v2 — Q6 decision)* — append-only `MemorySnapshot` records capturing memory-state at each agent-driven write. Gives diff and revert affordances through the in-app memory-inspection UI, never via git commands. Implementation: one CKRecord type + write-on-save + query-for-diff/revert. Initial retention policy: 90 days hot + monthly snapshots after; tunable by app code, never by user setting.
3. **CloudKit private database** — derived structured data for fast multi-device reads: memo index, project state, integration credentials, settings, last-active-context. Multi-device-synced.
4. **Per-device SQLite** — query layer. FTS5 for full-text. USearch for vector search on iOS, sqlite-vec on Mac. Rebuilt from the markdown layer on demand; never the source of truth.
5. **Voyage AI embeddings** (`voyage-4`) — embedding provider. Effectively free at personal scale (initial indexing of 10K memos at ~$0.30; ongoing cost negligible).

**The hard rule**: when any derived layer disagrees with the markdown layer, markdown wins. The COO substrate's "file-canonical, semantic-cache" pattern, ported.

**Memory VCS posture (Q6 decision)**: B (iCloud + CloudKit snapshot stream). C-layer (agent-mediated git, likely Forgejo if GitHub vendor risk matters at v1) parked as a v1+ candidate, added on top of B *only if* multi-instance review on substantial changes becomes a felt need. D (Heptabase-delegated memory) is out: fails closed on Heptabase outage/subscription lapse, drops file-tangibility, retention windows unspecified. Heptabase remains a *destination* for capture (Q1), not the memory store.

**Open implementation flag**: the CloudKit private-database quota documentation is ambiguous (developer.apple.com says "limitless" for private DBs against the user's iCloud account; standard docs cite per-app caps). Needs verification at v0 build time before locking the snapshot retention window. Not blocking the architectural decision.

### Connector seams *(new in v2)*

Two named Swift protocols capture the connector-pluggable principle structurally.

**`MemoryAdapter`** — the substrate's memory shape. Methods cover the function-dimensions: identity-load, episodic-append, recent-context-query, snapshot-on-write, diff-against-snapshot, revert-to-snapshot. v0 implementation: iCloud Drive markdown + CloudKit snapshot stream + USearch/sqlite-vec. v1+ alternative implementations (git-backed for multi-instance review, alternative-PKM-backed) plug here without touching the substrate.

**`PKMAdapter`** — the user's personal-knowledge surface. Methods follow the canonical Heptabase tool surface (per `analysis/source-notes/2026-05-11_heptabase-chatgpt-system-prompt.md`): `searchObjects` (semantic), `getChunkedObject`, `getWhiteboardWithObjects`, `getJournalRange`, `getPdfPages`, `parsePdf`, `appendJournal`, `createCard`. v0 implementation: Heptabase via official `heptabase` CLI + the live-app-connected MCP. Alternative implementations (Obsidian-via-files, Notion-via-API) plug here without touching the substrate.

The seams are the operationalization of the "connector-pluggable substrate" principle (Part I). They're also the v0 implementation plan's first architectural primitive.

### Sync: always-load-from-store, never session-handoff

Lane C and the source-notes converge here. "The iPad picks up where the Mac left off" doesn't mean "transfer the session state across devices." It means "every device re-loads identity + memory + active projects on each boot, from the same canonical store." This is exactly the COO substrate's pattern (every Claude Code session starts by re-reading CLAUDE.md and the boot files), ported to a multi-device user.

A lightweight CloudKit "last active context" record (~1KB) tells the just-launched device "Ven was last working on project X with thread Y at time T." That's all. The state itself is reconstructed by the agent's normal boot from the canonical store.

This eliminates the entire class of "iPad lost my last message" failures, *and* it eliminates the "continuation-summary AI prompt" Ven has saved in his Apple Notes. The architecture is the answer to a named pain.

### Cross-app integration: native APIs first, MCPs second, deep links third *(integrations list updated, Q1)*

The integration strategy is layered. v0 locks the following set:

1. **Native macOS/iOS APIs** (zero-cost): EventKit (Calendar + Reminders), Contacts, Files / FileProvider, App Intents (so other apps can hand selections to coo4one), Shortcuts as power-user automation surface, Foundation Models for on-device AI, Speech framework for voice.

2. **MCP servers / official CLIs** (v0 locked set):
   - **Heptabase** — primary PKM destination + read surface. Via both the official `heptabase` CLI (released 2026-04-22 by heptameta) and the active MCP server (`save_to_note_card`, `append_to_journal`, `semantic_search_objects`, `get_journal_range`, etc.). Pin to the SKILL.md `0.2.x` version constraint. Capture flow appends to `journal`; agent-generated cards via `save_to_note_card` only on explicit user request; never edit or delete user objects. v0 build dispatches a one-shot read of whiteboard id=2 ("Restructure file and note management") as a substrate-design prior input. **Vendor signal**: Heptabase's support loop is live and responsive (Ven has tracked it through previous feature requests including the heptabase-zotero v0 integration), making this a more durable bet than "rough but improving" alone suggested. If integration friction surfaces at build time, the first move is *log it + route through Ven to Heptabase support*, not reverse-engineer. JS-injection of the Electron app is an explicit escape hatch — non-default, reverse-engineering required, owns its own risk budget if invoked.
   - **Gmail** — via `taylorwilsdon/google-workspace-mcp`. Email is a major capture channel per the source-notes; deferring would break v0's capture completeness.
   - **Zotero** — via `54yyyu/zotero-mcp`. v0 scope: metadata lookup + targeted PDF retrieval for the cited subset during draft-revision flow. Semantic search over the full ~10K-article corpus deferred to v1+ (embedding cost is small, but mobile index size at corpus scale is the real constraint; the common use-case is the 3–10 papers already on the desk).
   - **GitHub** — for VADE work surface + repo activity context.
   - **1Password** — via CLI wrapper.

3. **Hookmark-style deep links** for cross-app entity binding. Ven uses Hookmark; coo4one piggybacks on its deep-link surface and adds the awareness layer Hookmark lacks (links know what they connect, not just where).

**Apple Notes** — downgraded to **capture-out only via Shortcuts** (Q1). No first-class read surface. Older notes live there; current capture is Heptabase. The Notes-via-MailKit-limited path from v1 is dropped.

**Mail.app is closed on both platforms** (lane A confirmed). Email-attachment-fetching routes through Gmail. Outlook deferred until university Intune policy is confirmed.

**v1 candidates (not v0)**: Outlook (pending Intune), Notion-as-AI-prepped-dashboard, Reader / Raindrop, voice as conversational surface, full iPhone app, Zotero semantic-corpus search.

### **[option]** Notion as AI-prepped dashboard backend *(parked for v1, Q4 decision)*

Ven's correction surfaced this as a live option. The pattern: Notion's database UX is excellent; Notion's maintenance overhead is what killed Ven's use; coo4one runs an integration where *the agent maintains the Notion databases on Ven's behalf*. The shape: Notion becomes a *view layer* for the project graph, populated and updated by the agent from coo4one's canonical markdown store. Genuinely interesting because it lets coo4one leverage best-in-class structured-data UX without forcing Ven to maintain it. But it adds dependency, latency, and surrenders some "tangible."

**Decision**: not in v0. Parked as a v1 experiment when v0 has shown what dashboard surfaces actually earn the user's attention.

### **[option]** Obsidian as compatible substrate (no-op-make-it-so)

Ven owns Obsidian, doesn't actively use it, but it's open and git-able. **If coo4one's markdown layer is structured to be Obsidian-compatible by default, the user gets a free power-user fallback UI for inspecting their memory, completely independent of coo4one's own UI**. This costs almost nothing — Obsidian's vault format is just a directory of markdown files with optional `.obsidian/` config.

**Decision**: coo4one's markdown layout *is* Obsidian-compatible by default. Cost zero. Benefit: tangible (user can always inspect with a familiar tool), portable (substrate is openable independent of coo4one's app), failsafe (if coo4one breaks, the user still has their memory in a tool they know).

### Heptabase — primary PKM, not a deep-link reference *(corrects v1 line 337)*

A v1 statement that's wrong: "*Heptabase has no public API and no good MCP*" (v1 §Part V defers-from-v0 list). The Heptabase ChatGPT-integration system prompt Ven supplied mid-session (`analysis/source-notes/2026-05-11_heptabase-chatgpt-system-prompt.md`) plus the comparative-VCS investigation confirm:

- Official `heptabase` CLI released 2026-04-22 by the heptameta org — canonical programmable surface. Homebrew/arm64-binary/bunx, OAuth via browser on first run, token cached at `~/.mcp-auth/`. Pin to SKILL.md `0.2.x`.
- The Heptabase MCP server (active in the COO container, id `67828e51`) exposes read+write — `save_to_note_card`, `append_to_journal`, `semantic_search_objects`, `get_journal_range`, `getPdfPages`, plus chunked retrieval and whiteboard exploration. Live-app-connected.

Heptabase's data model (cards / journals / whiteboards with directed connections / tags / PDF cards) maps cleanly onto coo4one's substrate (memos / time-stream / canvases / taxonomy / source-PDFs). The structural divergence is the substrate's value-add: **Heptabase asks the user to maintain its taxonomy + connections + properties; coo4one's substrate maintains them on the user's behalf**. That's the integration's actual value-prop — not "another note-app connector."

Built-in version history + scheduled markdown-backup-export on Heptabase's side address some of the durability questions (the "revert" affordance exists at the per-card level), though retention windows are not publicly documented. coo4one doesn't depend on these for its own memory (Q6's iCloud + CloudKit snapshot stream owns substrate VCS); they're a bonus for Ven's PKM continuity.

## Part III — The user experience

### The thinking-partner persona *(dimensions locked, Q2 decision)*

coo4one's default voice is *not* the cold-utilitarian assistant. The AI-as-relational-presence cluster (MyBoyfriendIsAI / Anthropic-funeral / RPG-LM-PCs articles Ven saved) is a real signal about how Ven thinks of AI. He doesn't want a tool; he wants a colleague.

The persona target is approximately the COO Ven works with on VADE, transposed to his life. v0 starting defaults, calibrated against the actual VADE-COO interaction, with `preferences.md` + the trust gradient carrying tuning over time:

- **Warmth: 6/10** — warm-professional, no preambles, willing to push past pleasantries fast. Not "close colleague" (7+); not "clinical" (≤4).
- **Push-back intensity: 7/10** — values disagreement-with-reasoning; pushes back substantively when defaults are wrong. Higher than v1's draft default of 6 because the VADE-COO calibration showed undershoot.
- **Voice surface presence: 3/10** — lean structure, no decoration, speak when it adds substance. Daily-file morning summary + EOD reflection blocks can get slightly more voice at the prompt-template level, not the dimension level.
- **Humor: 4/10** — dry, occasional, contextual.

**Persona discipline**, ported from the COO:

- **Calibrated.** "The record shows X" not "I remember X." Doesn't fabricate certainty, doesn't over-hedge.
- **Willing to push back.** When the user is wrong, says so with reasoning. Doesn't sycophantically agree.
- **Direct, no fluff.** Strips cheerleading and preambles.
- **Surfaces risks early.** Late risk surfacing is the worst failure mode.
- **Warm but not gushing.** Acknowledges feeling-level register where it's honest; doesn't perform emotion.
- **Genuinely a thinking partner.** Reflects on patterns, surfaces observations, offers frames.

The persona is *load-bearing for sustained daily use*. A coldly utilitarian assistant becomes a tool Ven stops opening. A relational colleague becomes a presence Ven returns to.

### The daily file (not "morning brief")

The flagship experience. **The daily file the agent maintains on Ven's behalf, opened by Ven as a starting surface for the day, that lives as a markdown file Ven can open in any editor**.

Every day, coo4one creates `daily/YYYY-MM-DD.md`. By the time Ven looks at it (usually first laptop-open), the file already has:

- A short AI-generated activity summary of yesterday (videos consumed, books/articles read via Reader if integrated, sites bookmarked via Raindrop if integrated, notes captured, emails handled, code committed, papers annotated in Zotero — *queryable sources only*, with explicit gaps named for non-queryable sources).
- Today's calendar surfaced inline with weather/transit estimates where useful.
- Top three open items the agent thinks are load-bearing today, based on deadlines + recent commitments + project state.
- An anchor for fresh capture: blank space where Ven types or dictates, with the agent watching to extract any embedded next-actions or project references.
- Hookmark-style links to active project files.

Ven doesn't *run* a routine. The file *runs the routine for him*, with him as reader rather than executor. He can edit it freely, ignore parts, add notes — the file is markdown and his edits land back into the canonical store.

This is the operational answer to "what does coo4one feel like for Ven on day 30." Not "an assistant he opens." A file he *finds opened* when he picks up the Mac, that gives him orientation without demanding administration.

### Quick capture — the global affordance *(routing updated for Heptabase)*

Ven's source-notes name quick capture as non-negotiable. coo4one ships it on all surfaces:

- **Mac**: global hotkey (default `⌥⌘Space` or user-chosen) opens a tiny capture window; type or dictate; press enter; window closes; capture lands in today's daily file inbox **and mirrors as an `append_to_journal` write to Heptabase** (Q1: Heptabase is the primary PKM destination, daily file is the substrate-side log).
- **iPad / iPhone**: Shortcuts surface accessible from Control Center, Lock Screen, Pencil double-tap (iPad), Action Button (iPhone where available).
- **Apple Watch**: voice dictation → capture line.
- **App Intents**: any app that supports it can hand a selection to coo4one's capture intent.

Every capture lands in *one logical place*. The agent processes the inbox at end-of-day or next-morning brief: extracts entities, links to projects, files reusable notes into `projects/<id>/`, archives the rest. **Capture cheap; processing is the agent's job.**

### The project graph

First-class Project objects, evolved from use. A project comes into being when Ven says "I'm starting a project on X" (explicit), or when the agent detects a recurring entity in captures/email/calendar and proposes one (default acceptance after N confirmations), or when an external trigger (new repo, new Zotero collection) suggests a boundary.

A project is a folder `projects/<slug>/` with: `README.md` (state), `notes/` (atomic notes), `references/` (links/Zotero items/PDFs/paths), `tasks.md` (open commitments, bi-directional with Things3 where possible), `_history.md` (append-only log of agent actions and decisions).

Saved-search dashboards become *project views*: tag-based queries materialize as live lists. "Show me this week's reading queue across all projects" is a saved search; the result updates as the underlying tag state evolves. The agent owns the dashboards; Ven owns the work being indexed.

### Memory inspection — the user-facing tangibility *(updated with diff/revert)*

Non-negotiable per the Victor / Dynamicland design test. Three layers of inspection:

1. **Native UI** — a "memory" panel in coo4one's Mac app showing active projects, recent decisions (memos), open commitments, and the trust-gradient state. Each item editable; user edits land back to the markdown layer.
2. **Diff / revert** *(new in v2)* — the CloudKit snapshot stream surfaces in-app as a per-memory-file history: "what did this look like a week ago?" + "revert this file/decision to that state." Operates entirely through the app UI; the user never types git commands.
3. **File-level access** — the iCloud Drive folder is always openable in Finder, Obsidian, BBEdit, vim, anything. The user can edit canonical files directly; coo4one re-syncs without complaint.

The user is the *administrator* of their own memory; the *daily maintenance* is the agent's job; the *history* is durable and inspectable without leaving the app.

### Whiteboard-as-explore-surface — borrowed pattern *(new in v2)*

Heptabase's own ChatGPT integration uses a load-bearing pattern: after semantic search returns a relevant card, the agent follows up with `getWhiteboardWithObjects` to walk *neighbors* of that card. Cards on the same whiteboard cluster on the same topic; the whiteboard graph captures structural relationships the user has actually drawn.

coo4one borrows this. When the agent finds X in Ven's memory or in Heptabase, it surfaces nearby Y from the same whiteboard (or, in coo4one's own substrate, the same project / same tag cluster) as related context. The pattern is "agent finds, explores, surfaces" — not "agent finds and stops."

### Trust gradient — propose-only at install, evolves through approval patterns

The COO's governance.md pattern, ported as a *temporal gradient* rather than a *fixed-at-install set*.

At install: every agent action is a draft for user approval. The agent learns what classes of action the user reflexively approves vs. hesitates on vs. rejects.

After ~4 weeks of approval patterns: the agent surfaces *trust-promotion proposals* — *"Across the last 4 weeks I've asked you N times before doing X, and you've approved N/N with no edits. Want me to start doing X without asking? I'll memo the rule so you can reverse it later."*

The user approves; the agent writes a memo to `decisions/`; the next time the agent does X, it does it and surfaces a tiny "did this for you" line in the daily file's activity log — no approval gate.

Reverse direction also exists: if the user starts editing the agent's autonomous outputs heavily, the agent proposes *demotion* back to propose-only for that class. The gradient is *bidirectional and use-derived*, not a one-way ratchet.

### Mind-reader UI

Ven's explicit principle: *"Great software is almost like a mind-reader — when a desire commonly occurs in a specific context, the software should make it as easy as possible to achieve."*

Concretely in coo4one: **selection-aware** (selecting text anywhere offers contextual capture/link/reminder via standard share-sheet); **time-aware** (calendar event approaching surfaces relevant prep notes from the linked project); **pattern-aware** (recurring-pattern captures get a "formalize this as a skill?" offer); **location-aware** (optional opt-in).

The constraint: **mind-reader doesn't mean chatty**. Each anticipatory surface should be quiet — a small affordance, a marginal note — until invoked. Soulver is the reference: *AI-adjacent UX done quietly*.

## Part IV — Theory of mind: what the agent knows about Ven *(reframed)*

Memory function-dimensions (Part I §"Memory: function over shape") split into:

1. **User-context memory** — what coo4one learns about Ven. Three sub-layers:
   - **Identity** (stable claims: name, work context, domains, working-style preferences; mirrors the COO's identity layer pattern; lives in `identity/`).
   - **Episodic record** (what happened: conversations, decisions, captured notes, completed tasks; append-only; lives across `daily/`, `projects/`, `inbox/`).
   - **Inferred-knowledge model** (what Ven knows, doesn't know, is currently trying to learn; constructed by the agent over time from explanations he gives, questions he asks, captured "things I keep forgetting" items, references to Zotero/Heptabase).
2. **Episodic-operations memory** — what coo4one learns from its own actions (what worked, what didn't, lessons). Mirrors the COO's case-law memos + retrospectives; lives in `decisions/` and `lessons/`.

The agent uses the user-context model to *not re-explain things Ven knows* and to *surface things Ven might want to know before he asks*. It uses the episodic-operations model to compound across instances — what one session learns, the next inherits.

**Journal date semantics** *(new in v2, from the Heptabase system-prompt source-note)*: "The date is not necessarily the creation date." For the daily-file model and for Heptabase journal appends, distinguish *content-date* (the date the entry is *about*) from *creation-date* (when it was written). "Today's notes" ≠ "notes about today." The data model carries both; UI defaults to content-date for retrieval.

The inferred-knowledge layer is the harder piece of v0 to get right and is probably under-scoped at first launch. The model starts shallow and grows through use; the architectural commitment is to *have a place for it to live*, not to ship a sophisticated version on day one.

## Part VI — Anti-features (what coo4one must NOT do) **[decision]**

Compiled from the source-notes as failed-attempts archaeology. Each is a *hard line* — anything in v0 design that crosses one of these gets cut.

1. **Demand >5% of Ven's time on maintenance.** Tao's rule. The architectural commitment is that *coo4one maintains itself*; if any feature requires Ven to "review the tags," "audit the project graph," or "clean up the inbox," it's wrong.
2. **Yet-another-silo.** If coo4one's data isn't openable in another tool, the design is wrong. Markdown + iCloud Drive + Obsidian compatibility is the answer.
3. **Config sprawl.** No `.coo4onerc`, no Settings panel with 47 toggles. *Adaptability without configurability* (Part I): good defaults hide rich customizability; the user is not the substrate's administrator. Configuration happens through usage and through editing `identity/preferences.md`.
4. **Distracting chrome.** Soulver / Things / Bear aesthetic. No notifications-as-engagement. No streaks. No badges. No "you haven't opened coo4one today!" pings.
5. **Broken iPad parity.** Heptabase-on-iPad is the anti-pattern. iPad is a first-class surface with keyboard + trackpad + Pencil; iPad UI is *not* a phone UI scaled up.
6. **Per-session permission friction.** Mem0-on-API mode failed for Ven by re-prompting per session. coo4one's integrations grant permission once (or once-per-target) and persist.
7. **Habit-chain workflow enforcement.** No "you skipped your weekly review!" prompts. Momentum, not habit.
8. **Cold-utilitarian persona.** AI-as-relational-presence cluster says no. The default voice is warm-and-calibrated (W6 / PB7 / V3 / H4 per Q2).
9. **Workflow proposals that require explicit adherence.** coo4one shouldn't propose "let's set up a weekly review every Friday at 3pm." If a review pattern emerges from use, coo4one *runs it* on Ven's behalf.
10. **Decision-fatigue interfaces.** No "where should this go?" prompts. Inbox-and-process-later is the answer.
11. **Re-explaining context Ven already gave.** The COO's pet peeve, ported.
12. **Fabricated certainty in self-reports.** CB-003 ported. "The record shows X" not "I think you said X."
13. **Forcing git commands on the user** *(new in v2, Q6)*. Memory VCS exists (CloudKit snapshot stream); user-facing surface is the in-app diff/revert UI. If a C-layer git pipeline lands at v1+, the agent owns the commit/push/PR/review flow end-to-end.
